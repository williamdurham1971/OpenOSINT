What large US companies stopped and started warning investors about.

Every 10-K filed with the SEC has a Risk Factors section (Item 1A). This dataset compares it paragraph by paragraph against the prior year for 50 large US companies, three years back, marks each paragraph as new, removed or rewritten, and tracks twenty risk themes across all of them. The diff and the theme counts are computed in code from the original EDGAR filings; news checks and readings are added by Claude and cite their sources.

How it is built

  1. The four most recent 10-K filings per company are downloaded from SEC EDGAR, at no more than five requests per second.
  2. Item 1A is cut out of each filing. The table-of-contents entry, page numbers and hidden XBRL data are dropped. Filers that print the section under a bare “Risk Factors” heading are located by that heading and flagged in the viewer.
  3. Each paragraph of the newer year is matched to its closest paragraph in the older year. Unmatched paragraphs are new or removed; matched ones that differ beyond numbers and dates are rewritten.
  4. Twenty risk themes (tariffs, export controls, AI, cybersecurity, litigation, liquidity and so on) are counted by keyword in every section. A company signal fires when a theme appears, disappears or doubles; a market signal fires when at least a fifth of the comparable companies move the same way in the same fiscal year.
  5. Claude checks signals against public news with web search and writes a short reading. Only URLs returned by the search are kept, and each reading states whether the link is confirmed or plausible.
  6. Only changed paragraphs go to Claude for a category and a 1–5 materiality score; quotes are checked against the filing and flagged if absent.

Limits

A paragraph that was split or merged shows up as one removal plus new text. Keyword counts miss paraphrase and catch unrelated uses of a word, so a signal is a prompt to read the paragraphs, not a finding. Two filers place risk factors in an exhibit and are marked as not extracted. Readings are model judgements, not measurements, and nothing here is investment advice.
