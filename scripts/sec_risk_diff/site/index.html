What large US companies stopped and started warning investors about.

Every 10-K filed with the SEC has a Risk Factors section (Item 1A). This dataset compares it paragraph by paragraph against the prior year for 50 large US companies, three years back, and marks each paragraph as new, removed or rewritten. The diff is computed in code from the original EDGAR filings; a Claude assessment of each change is added on top.

How it is built

  1. The four most recent 10-K filings per company are downloaded from SEC EDGAR, at no more than five requests per second.
  2. Item 1A is cut out of each filing. The table-of-contents entry, page numbers and hidden XBRL data are dropped.
  3. Each paragraph of the newer year is matched to its closest paragraph in the older year. Unmatched paragraphs are new or removed; matched ones that differ beyond numbers and dates are rewritten.
  4. Only the changed paragraphs go to Claude, which assigns a category and a 1–5 materiality score and quotes the supporting text. Quotes are checked against the filing; a quote that is not in the text is flagged.

Limits

A paragraph that was split or merged shows up as one removal plus new text. Some filers place risk factors in an exhibit, and those years are marked as not extracted. Assessments are model judgements, not measurements, and nothing here is investment advice.
